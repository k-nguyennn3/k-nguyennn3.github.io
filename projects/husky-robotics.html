Husky Robotics: Rover Spectrometer

The spectrometer instrument package for UW's student Mars-rover team: mechanisms, packaging, and thermal control for analyzing soil samples in the field.

Spectrometer Engineer

The problem

The rover's instrumentation looks for signs of past biological activity in soil samples, and the spectrometer searches for kerogen as one of those indicators. Spectral readings are sensitive to heat, stray light, and vibration, and loosely packed soil scatters light unpredictably. Everything also had to share a 16 × 16 × 24 in envelope with the drill and chemical tests. The spectrometer box alone is 4 × 16 in.

My role

  • Designed and built a 3-actuator electromechanical mechanism, owning the full cycle from concept to field deployment.
  • Created a tamping mechanism that compresses each soil sample before measurement, eliminating random scattering for a more precise reading.
  • Developed and tested thermal management (heat sinks and thermoelectric coolers) to keep the sensor below 80 °F, avoiding temperature-related shifts in the spectra.
  • Integrated the assembly by routing wire harnesses and connectors within a compact chassis enclosure, then bench tested it.
  • Worked the spectrometer into the full instrumentation CAD with five other sub-teams, iterating designs on both sides until everything fit, while cutting the subsystem's weight.
  • Machined and 3D-printed prototypes, applying DFM/DFA with shop machinists to cut assembly rework by 20% over 8 builds.
